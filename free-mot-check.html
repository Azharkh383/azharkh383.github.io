UK VEHICLE CHECK • PRACTICAL GUIDE

Free MOT check: what to look for

A free MOT check is useful for reviewing a vehicle’s recorded MOT status and test history. Enter the registration into the official service to retrieve the vehicle-specific record. This website is not yet connected to live MOT data, so its form cannot provide real results at present.

01

1. Check the plate

Enter the registration exactly as shown on the number plate. If the official result appears to describe another vehicle, stop and check the registration again.

02

2. Confirm status and expiry

Look for the latest test result and expiry date. A valid MOT does not guarantee that a vehicle is roadworthy in every respect today; drivers must still keep it safe and road legal.

03

3. Review failures and advisories

Failures show defects that prevented a pass at that test. Advisories identify items the tester noted for attention; they are not a guarantee that the item remains unchanged or that it has been repaired.

04

4. Verify and keep notes

Use the official record, save the information you need and ask the owner or seller to explain anything that does not match the vehicle or its paperwork.

What a free MOT check does not tell you

  • It is not a full vehicle history or mechanical inspection.
  • It does not by itself confirm road tax, current insurance, outstanding finance or stolen status.
  • A missing record can have several explanations; verify through the official service rather than assuming the vehicle has no MOT.

For vehicle-specific information, verify the record with the official GOV.UK MOT history service .

STEP-BY-STEP GUIDE

How to check a vehicle’s MOT online

Keep the process simple: use the registration number, review the record carefully, and verify anything important before making a decision.

01

Enter the registration

Use the vehicle’s current UK registration mark exactly as shown on its number plate. If you are checking a car you may buy, compare the registration with the vehicle in front of you.

02

Review the latest test

Look for the latest MOT test date, result and recorded expiry date. A pass is a record of the test at that time, not a guarantee that the vehicle is safe today.

03

Read defects and advisories

Pay attention to failures, major or dangerous defects, and advisories. Advisories may not have caused a failure, but they can identify items that need monitoring or repair.

04

Verify before acting

Cross-check important details using the official GOV.UK service and ask for invoices or repair evidence where relevant.

READ THE RECORD

What to look for in the results

MOT expiry date

Check the date against your plans and confirm the record with the official service. Do not rely on an old screenshot or a seller’s verbal assurance alone.

Recorded mileage

Compare readings across test years. Mileage can vary for legitimate reasons, but a sudden decrease or unusual gap should be checked against service invoices and the vehicle’s odometer.

Test outcome

A pass means the vehicle met the applicable MOT requirements on the test date. A fail means defects were found; check the details and any subsequent retest record.

Advisories

Advisories are not necessarily immediate failures. They can still point to wear, corrosion or other items that may need attention and cost money later.

What a free MOT check cannot tell you

An MOT record is useful, but it is only one part of checking a vehicle.

  • It is not a full mechanical inspection or a guarantee of present roadworthiness.
  • It does not automatically confirm road tax, insurance, outstanding finance or stolen status.
  • It may not explain every repair, service visit or incident in the vehicle’s life.
  • For a purchase, combine the record with a physical inspection, a test drive and appropriate independent checks.
