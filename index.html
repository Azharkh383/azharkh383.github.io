A clearer way to understand a vehicle’s MOT record

Use this independent guide to understand MOT status, past tests and the warning signs worth checking before you buy or keep a vehicle. The site is a front end and does not currently fetch live records; use the official checker for the vehicle-specific result.

01

Start with the registration

A UK registration number helps the official service find a vehicle’s MOT record. Check the plate carefully and compare the make and model returned with the vehicle you are viewing.

02

Read the whole story

One pass result is only a snapshot. Review the sequence of test dates, mileages, failures and advisories to understand what has changed over time.

03

Use MOT history as one part of your decision

An MOT record is not a full mechanical inspection and does not prove that a vehicle has no finance, theft marker, insurance write-off or hidden damage. Consider a professional inspection and separate checks where appropriate.

Your quick MOT-check checklist

  • Confirm the registration and vehicle identity match.
  • Check the latest result and MOT expiry date.
  • Read mileage entries in date order and ask about any unexpected drop.
  • Review repeated advisories and previous failures.
  • Verify the result on GOV.UK before relying on it.

A practical used-car MOT checklist

Before you commit to buying, work through these simple checks.

  1. Match the registration and vehicle identity to the car and its paperwork.
  2. Check the official MOT history and note the latest result and expiry date.
  3. Compare mileage readings across test dates and ask about any discrepancies.
  4. Review failures and advisories, and request evidence for significant repairs.
  5. Inspect the car in person and consider an independent mechanical inspection.
  6. Check other relevant details separately; MOT history does not cover every legal or financial check.
