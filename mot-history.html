How to Read an MOT Check Result

An MOT check is useful for understanding a vehicle's recorded test history. Read the details in context and verify important information using the official service.

Check the current status

Look at the recorded MOT status and expiry date. Do not assume an old pass means the vehicle has a current MOT today.

Compare past test results

Review dates and pass or fail outcomes. Repeated failures or advisories can help you identify questions to ask the seller.

Review mileage readings

Compare readings across test dates. An inconsistency may need investigation, but mileage records alone do not prove tampering.

Understand advisories

Advisories identify issues noted by the tester that may need attention. Ask a qualified mechanic to assess any concerns before buying.

UK VEHICLE CHECK GUIDE

How to read MOT history

MOT history helps you see how a vehicle performed at previous annual tests. Look at the result, test date, mileage and any defects or advisories recorded on each visit.

01

What to review

A repeated advisory may indicate an issue that has not been addressed. A sudden mileage drop or an unusually large gap between readings deserves a closer look, but records can contain errors and should be checked in context. Ask for invoices and maintenance records, and arrange an independent inspection for a used-car purchase.

02

Know the limits

An MOT pass only reflects the test result at that time. It does not confirm service history, accident history, outstanding finance or the vehicle’s current mechanical condition.

03

Next step

For official records, visit GOV.UK MOT history . Keep a copy of relevant records and ask the seller to explain anything that does not match the vehicle or its paperwork.

Guidance only. Always verify important details using official sources.

UK VEHICLE CHECK • PRACTICAL GUIDE

Understand a vehicle’s MOT history

MOT history is a timeline of recorded tests. It can help you see whether a vehicle passed or failed, the mileage recorded at each test, and the defects or advisories recorded by the tester.

01

Look at the timeline, not just the latest pass

A recent pass tells you the outcome of one test. Older results can reveal repeated issues, changes in condition or long gaps that deserve a question.

02

Compare mileage in date order

Mileage recorded at MOT tests would normally be expected to increase. A lower reading can result from a recording error, an instrument change or another issue; it is a reason to investigate, not proof of tampering.

03

Understand defects and advisories

Failure items describe problems found at the test. Advisories are observations that may need monitoring or repair. Check whether the same issue appears repeatedly and ask for invoices or repair evidence.

04

Check the record’s coverage

For cars, motorcycles and vans in Great Britain, official records are generally available for tests from 2005 onwards. HGVs, trailers, buses and coaches have records from 2018 onwards; Northern Ireland has a separate service and records from 2017.

Questions worth asking a seller

  • Can you explain any mileage drop or unusual gap?
  • Do you have receipts for previous failures and advisory repairs?
  • Has the vehicle had recent work that is not reflected in the MOT record yet?
  • Can I arrange an independent inspection before purchase?

HISTORY GUIDE

How to read an MOT timeline

Read each test as a dated snapshot. The pattern across several years often tells you more than one result on its own.

Compare test dates

Check the order and spacing of tests. A missing year or an unusual gap is a reason to ask questions, not proof of a problem by itself.

Compare mileage readings

Put the mileage from each test in chronological order. A steady increase is common, while a drop or a large unexplained jump should be verified with paperwork and the odometer.

Follow failures and retests

A failed test can be followed by repairs and a pass or retest. Read the dates and defect descriptions together to understand what was recorded.

Notice repeated advisories

The same advisory appearing repeatedly may suggest that an issue has not been addressed. Ask the seller or keeper for an explanation and evidence of repairs.

Red flags worth investigating

These are prompts for further checks, not automatic proof that a vehicle is unsuitable.

Mileage inconsistency

Ask for service records, previous invoices and a clear explanation of any apparent mileage discrepancy.

Repeated safety-related defects

Look for repeated issues involving tyres, brakes, steering, suspension, visibility or structural condition and request repair evidence.

Recent MOT pass before sale

A recent pass is useful but does not replace an independent inspection. Condition can change after the test.

Before buying a used vehicle

  • Match the registration and vehicle identity to the records and paperwork.
  • Check the MOT history on the official GOV.UK service.
  • Ask about failures, advisories and any repairs carried out.
  • Compare the odometer with recorded readings and service history.
  • Arrange an independent inspection if you are uncertain about the vehicle’s condition.

MOT history is not a complete service history and cannot confirm every aspect of a vehicle’s legal or mechanical status.
