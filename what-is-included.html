What Does an MOT Check Show?

An MOT check focuses on recorded MOT information. The exact details depend on the vehicle, the records available and the data source used. Use these sections to understand what to look for when reviewing an MOT record.

MOT status and expiry

Check whether a vehicle has a recorded current MOT and when the certificate expires. Always confirm the latest status before driving or buying.

Past MOT test results

Review test dates and whether each recorded test passed or failed. A previous pass describes the test result at that time, not the vehicle’s condition today.

Recorded mileage

Compare odometer readings in date order. A surprising change can warrant further checks, but MOT readings alone cannot prove mileage fraud.

Failures and advisories

Read recorded defects and advisories to understand issues identified during a test and items that may need attention.

Safety recalls

Some MOT history records may include a safety recall indication, depending on the manufacturer and the information available. Verify recall information with the appropriate official source.

Test centre information

Some test-location details may require additional information, such as the 11-digit number from the vehicle log book for eligible tests in Great Britain.

What an MOT Check Does Not Tell You

An MOT history is not a complete vehicle history report and is not a guarantee that a vehicle is mechanically sound. It does not automatically provide finance status, insurance write-off history, stolen-vehicle checks, full service records or every vehicle specification. Only describe these as available if your service has a verified source for them.

An MOT test records the condition of testable items at the time of the test. Before buying a used vehicle, consider an independent mechanical inspection and verify the vehicle details against its documents.

Common MOT Failure Areas

Depending on the vehicle and test, recorded problems can involve tyres, lights, brakes, steering, suspension or emissions. If an MOT record lists a defect, read its exact description rather than relying on a general summary. Ask a qualified mechanic to assess safety concerns before driving or buying the vehicle.

Record item What it means What to do next
Pass The vehicle passed that MOT test. Check the current expiry date and remember condition can change after the test.
Fail The vehicle did not pass that test. Read the recorded defects and check for a later retest or pass record.
Advisory An item was noted for attention or monitoring. Ask whether it has been repaired and arrange an inspection if needed.
Mileage reading The odometer value recorded at a test. Compare readings chronologically and investigate inconsistencies.

Used-Car MOT Check: A Practical Checklist

MOT history is one part of a used-car review. Use the record to prepare questions, then verify the details with official records and an independent inspection.

01

Compare mileage in date order

Look for a logical progression between tests. If a reading decreases or jumps unexpectedly, ask for an explanation and supporting service records.

02

Read the exact defects

Review failures and advisories individually. A short summary can miss the context of the tester’s recorded wording.

03

Check the latest record

Look for a later retest or pass after a failure. Confirm the current MOT status and expiry date with GOV.UK before making a decision.

An MOT pass does not guarantee a vehicle’s present condition, and MOT history does not replace checks for outstanding finance, insurance write-off status, theft or service history.

MOT Rules, Failures and Next Steps

Use this practical MOT guide to understand what to do when an MOT is due, a vehicle fails, or an MOT record is missing. Rules can differ for Northern Ireland and some vehicle classes, so verify the details with the official service.

When is my MOT due?

Most cars, motorcycles and light vehicles need their first MOT by the third anniversary of first registration and then annually. Some vehicle types need an earlier first test. Check the official record for the vehicle’s due date rather than estimating from its age.

Official guide: when to get an MOT

What happens if a vehicle fails its MOT?

Read the exact defects on the refusal notice. A vehicle with dangerous defects must not be driven until the problems are fixed. Whether you can take a failed vehicle away depends on its existing MOT validity and whether dangerous defects were recorded.

Official guide: MOT test results

Can I drive with an expired MOT?

Generally, you must not drive or park a vehicle on the road without a valid MOT when one is required. Exceptions include travelling to a pre-arranged MOT appointment or to have defects repaired, but the vehicle must still be roadworthy. Check the official rules before travelling.

Official guidance on expired MOTs

MOT exemptions and first MOT date

Some vehicles may be exempt from MOT testing, including certain historic vehicles, but exemptions have conditions and can depend on vehicle modifications. Do not assume a vehicle is exempt just because it is old; verify eligibility with official guidance.

Official historic vehicle guidance

MOT retest after repairs

Retest arrangements and fees depend on where repairs are carried out and how soon the vehicle is returned. Keep the test paperwork, repair invoices and any retest result together, then check the latest MOT record for an updated result.

Official guide: MOT retests

Other checks before buying a car

An MOT history does not automatically confirm vehicle tax, outstanding finance, insurance write-off status or stolen status. These require separate checks and data sources. UK Vehicle Check does not claim to provide those results unless a relevant service is implemented.

MOT Defect Categories Explained

Understanding the wording of an MOT result helps you decide what to ask a seller or mechanic. The official record and test paperwork take priority over any simplified summary.

Term Plain-English meaning What to do
Pass The vehicle passed that test; minor defects or advisories may still be recorded. Check the current expiry date and review advisories.
Minor defect A defect that does not cause the test to fail, but should be monitored or addressed. Read the tester’s exact wording and plan any maintenance.
Major defect A more serious problem that results in an MOT failure. Arrange repairs and follow the test centre’s advice about whether the vehicle can be driven.
Dangerous defect A defect presenting a direct and immediate risk to road safety or a serious environmental risk. Do not drive until the issue is repaired; follow official guidance.
Advisory An item noted for attention or monitoring that did not itself cause a fail. Ask whether it has since been repaired and consider an inspection.

For current official rules, consult GOV.UK MOT test results and the MOT inspection manual .

What is included in an MOT history check?

The information available depends on the vehicle and the records held by the official service. This guide describes common MOT-history fields; it does not claim that this demo website currently retrieves them live.

01

Test result and date

The record may show when the test took place and whether the vehicle passed or failed.

02

Expiry date

Where available, the record shows when the current MOT certificate expires. Check the official record for the latest status.

03

Recorded mileage

The odometer reading noted at each test can help you review the vehicle’s recorded mileage pattern over time.

04

Failures and advisories

The history can list failure reasons and minor problems or advisories noted by the tester. Their presence should be interpreted in the context of the date of the test.

05

Vehicle and test details

Depending on vehicle type and record availability, information may include the test number and basic vehicle details. Some details, such as test location, may require information from the V5C log book.

06

What is not included

MOT history alone does not confirm road tax, insurance, outstanding finance, theft status, write-off history, service history or a full condition assessment.

MOT terminology at a glance

  • Pass: the vehicle met the test requirements at that test.
  • Fail: one or more defects meant it did not pass.
  • Advisory: an item was noted for attention or monitoring.
  • Mileage reading: the odometer value recorded at the test.

For vehicle-specific information, verify the record with the official GOV.UK MOT history service .

Information commonly shown in MOT history

What is not included or guaranteed

Not a full service history

MOT records do not list every service, repair, accident or maintenance job. Request invoices and service documentation separately.

Not a mechanical warranty

A pass applies to the test date and scope. It does not guarantee that a vehicle will remain fault-free or safe indefinitely.

Not every ownership check

Do not assume an MOT check confirms insurance, road tax, outstanding finance, theft status, write-off history or ownership.

Records may have limits

Coverage and detail depend on the official records available. Verify unexpected gaps or discrepancies with the official service.
