What is the difference between an MOT check and an MOT history check?

An MOT status check helps you find out whether a vehicle has a current MOT and when it expires. An MOT history check focuses on previous MOT tests, including recorded pass or fail results, mileage and advisories where available. Use the official GOV.UK service to verify government MOT records.

Used Car MOT Check: Mileage History, Failures and Advisories

Before buying a used car, use an MOT history check to review previous test results, recorded mileage, MOT failures and advisories. Repeated defects or unusual changes in mileage readings may be reasons to ask the seller more questions or arrange an independent vehicle inspection. An MOT history check is one useful part of a used-car check, but it does not replace a mechanical inspection or confirm a vehicle's full history.

What can cause a vehicle to fail an MOT test?

Common MOT failure areas include tyres, lights, brakes, steering, suspension and exhaust emissions. Review any recorded MOT defects and advisories carefully, and ask a qualified mechanic to assess concerns before you buy. The records available depend on the vehicle and the data source.

Can MOT mileage history help identify clocking?

MOT mileage history lets you compare odometer readings recorded at different tests. An unexpected drop or inconsistency can warrant further checks, although mileage records alone cannot prove that a vehicle has been clocked.

Is an MOT check the same as a full vehicle history check?

No. An MOT check focuses on MOT status and test history, including results, recorded mileage and advisories where available. A broader vehicle history check may cover categories such as finance, write-off records or vehicle specifications. Do not assume those details are included here unless the service explicitly provides them.

Check MOT Status and Expiry Date Before You Buy

A vehicle MOT check can help you review the recorded test history before buying a used car. Compare previous MOT pass and fail results, recorded mileage and repeated advisories, and confirm the current MOT expiry date using an up-to-date data source.

What is the difference between an MOT status check and an MOT history check?

An MOT status check focuses on whether a vehicle has a current MOT and when it expires. An MOT history check helps you review earlier test dates, pass or fail outcomes, mileage readings and recorded defects or advisories where available.

Can I check MOT history using a number plate?

Yes. The UK government’s MOT history service uses the vehicle registration number. This independent website is intended to help users review available information, but its lookup must be connected to a functioning data source before it can return real vehicle records.

Where can I verify an MOT result?

UK VEHICLE CHECK • PRACTICAL GUIDE

How to read an MOT report

A useful MOT report should make the official record easier to understand without pretending to know more than the source provides. Always distinguish recorded facts from interpretation and verify important details with the official service.

01

Vehicle summary

Start by matching the registration and basic vehicle details. If the returned details do not match the car, do not continue on the assumption that the report is correct.

02

Current status and expiry

Check the latest recorded test outcome and expiry date. The expiry date is a useful reminder, but it is not a substitute for checking the vehicle’s condition or legal requirements.

03

Test-by-test results

Read each test in chronological order. Note pass/fail outcomes, dates, mileage and any listed defects. Repeated failures or advisories may help you decide what to ask next.

04

Mileage and anomalies

Compare the recorded mileage entries. Treat unexpected changes as prompts for verification, and ask for service records or other evidence before drawing conclusions.

05

Limitations

MOT data covers test records. It does not by itself establish ownership, outstanding finance, insurance, theft status, accident history or the full mechanical condition of a vehicle.

A sensible report workflow

  • Verify the vehicle identity against the registration.
  • Read the latest test result first, then work backwards.
  • Highlight repeat advisories and unexplained mileage changes.
  • Ask for evidence and consider an independent inspection.
  • Confirm details with the official GOV.UK record.

REPORT EXPLAINED

Understand each part of an MOT report

Use the report as a structured record of the inspection, then decide what needs follow-up.

Pass

The vehicle passed the relevant MOT test on that date. It does not mean every component is new or that no maintenance will be needed later.

Fail

The test identified one or more defects that prevented a pass. Read each defect description and confirm what was repaired before relying on a later result.

Major and dangerous defects

These classifications indicate serious issues under MOT rules. Follow the official guidance and do not drive a vehicle when it is unsafe or unlawful to do so.

Advisory items

An advisory records an item that should be monitored or addressed. It may become more serious as wear progresses.

Minor defects

Where listed, minor defects should still be understood and maintained. Their presence does not mean the vehicle needs no attention.

Retest information

Where a retest is recorded, compare its date and outcome with the original test. Ask for receipts or workshop records for important repairs.

Turn the report into an action plan

  1. Prioritise safety. Start with brakes, tyres, steering, suspension, lights, visibility and structural defects.
  2. Confirm repairs. Ask for dated invoices or other evidence for significant work.
  3. Estimate future maintenance. Advisories can help you plan for likely upkeep, but obtain a qualified assessment for costs.
  4. Re-check the latest record. Use the official service to confirm that the displayed test history is current.

A report summary is not a substitute for a qualified mechanic’s inspection or the full official record.
